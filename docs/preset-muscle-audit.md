# Preset muscle audit

October 3, 2026. Reviewed all 79 built-in exercise presets; corrected 20 defaults and retained 59. The [audit record](preset-muscle-audit.json) includes the previous values, final recommendations, rationale, assumptions, evidence limits and source references for every preset.

Direct means a major intended target of the described movement. Indirect means meaningful assisting work. These roles are a practical counting convention; anatomy, coaching descriptions and acute muscle activation cannot prove an exact hypertrophy credit. The existing direct + half indirect calculation is unchanged. [Pelland et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w) supports fractional counting as a useful model, not a universal 50% stimulus for every exercise.

The app keeps fourteen broad regions. A group may represent only one participating muscle: calf assistance in leg curls means gastrocnemius, not soleus; forearm assistance in curls means brachioradialis, not every wrist muscle. Brachialis, hip flexors, serratus anterior and rotator-cuff muscles have no separate IDs. They are not relabeled as another group. Shoulders, core, glutes and upper back likewise do not imply equal work across subdivisions.

No database migration or deployment. Saved explicit muscle assignments, including empty assignments, keep priority. No routine, workout snapshot or history record is automatically rewritten. Historical records that lack assignments still resolve against the current catalog at read time, as before. In the exercise editor, **Use preset muscles** changes only the selected exercise in the local draft; keeping it requires Save program or Apply to session. Existing exact retries and save locks remain. Personal presets retain their saved definitions. The existing name editor intentionally clears draft muscle assignments when a name changes, including cosmetic edits, to avoid keeping another movement's targets; this behavior is unchanged.

## Corrections

- **Trap-bar deadlift** — Direct: Quads, Glutes; Indirect: Hamstrings, Lower back. Quads and glutes lead; hamstrings and lower back assist. Handle height and technique matter.
- **Overhead press** — Direct: Shoulders; Indirect: Triceps, Upper back. Conventional front overhead barbell press with normal scapular movement.
- **Seated dumbbell press** — Direct: Shoulders; Indirect: Triceps, Upper back. Seated upright dumbbell overhead press; no large incline-back lean.
- **Machine shoulder press** — Direct: Shoulders; Indirect: Triceps, Upper back. Upright overhead shoulder-press machine with normal scapular movement.
- **Biceps curl** — Direct: Biceps; Indirect: Forearms. Strict supinated barbell curl with upper arms stationary. Brachialis also contributes but has no separate app group.
- **Dumbbell curl** — Direct: Biceps; Indirect: Forearms. Strict supinated or supinating dumbbell curl; no shoulder swing. Brachialis also contributes but has no separate app group.
- **Preacher curl** — Direct: Biceps; Indirect: Forearms. Supinated curl with upper arms supported on preacher pad. Brachialis also contributes but has no separate app group.
- **Cable curl** — Direct: Biceps; Indirect: Forearms. Underhand low-pulley curl; upper arms stationary beside torso. Brachialis also contributes but has no separate app group.
- **Leg curl** — Direct: Hamstrings; Indirect: Calves. Hamstrings target; gastrocnemius assists knee flexion. This does not credit soleus.
- **Seated leg curl** — Direct: Hamstrings; Indirect: Calves. Hamstrings target; gastrocnemius assists. Seated position lengthens biarticular hamstrings.
- **Lateral raise** — Direct: Shoulders; Indirect: Upper back. Neutral-rotation lateral raise to shoulder height with normal scapular motion.
- **Cable lateral raise** — Direct: Shoulders; Indirect: Upper back. Cable-resisted lateral arm raise; neutral rotation and normal scapular motion.
- **Rear-delt fly** — Direct: Shoulders; Indirect: Upper back. Near shoulder-height fly with fixed softly bent elbows and natural scapular retraction. Upper-back assistance decreases if shoulder blades are deliberately held still.
- **Reverse pec deck** — Direct: Shoulders; Indirect: Upper back. Assumes chest support and natural scapular retraction while opening the arms near shoulder height. Deliberately fixed shoulder blades reduce upper-back assistance.
- **Face pull** — Direct: Shoulders, Upper back; Indirect: Biceps. Eye-height rope pull with elbows bending and travelling outward/back.
- **Cable fly** — Direct: Chest; Indirect: Shoulders. Chest-height cable fly with a consistent slight elbow bend.
- **Pec deck** — Direct: Chest; Indirect: Shoulders. Chest fly machine moving upper arms inward with elbows held at a consistent bend.
- **Dumbbell fly** — Direct: Chest; Indirect: Shoulders. Supported lying dumbbell fly with elbow bend maintained.
- **Bird dog** — Direct: Abs / core, Lower back; Indirect: Glutes. Quadruped opposite arm/leg extension with deliberate neutral-spine and pelvic control.
- **Chest-supported high row** — Direct: Upper back, Shoulders; Indirect: Biceps. Assumes a chest-supported horizontal row with elbows out near shoulder height, emphasizing rear deltoids and scapular retractors. High-to-low lat machines use a different arm path; choose explicit muscle assignments for that variation.
